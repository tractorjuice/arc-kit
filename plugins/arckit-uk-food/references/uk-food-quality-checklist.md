# UK food safety overlay: checks before the summary

Every command in this overlay runs these checks after its last write and before it shows its summary. Each one is something you can look at: a helper's exit code, a count from `status --json`, a line in a file. If a check fails, put it right as it says, then look again.

For this overlay's documents these checks take the place of the Common Checks in ArcKit's general quality checklist. The body of each document is printed by the helper, and a general rule about headings or tables must never be met by changing it.

## Every document a command writes

1. **`check` passed.** The last `check --doc CODE --project PROJECT --report FILE` for the file as written exited 0. If it exited 1, the document was written again from a fresh render, never edited by hand between the markers.
2. **The body is the render, whole.** The file holds exactly one `UK-FOOD:BEGIN` line and one `UK-FOOD:END` line, and between them the render output and nothing else. Nothing from the pack appears outside them, and no heading outside them is a section's title or starts with a safe method's identifier and title.
3. **The header follows `status --json`.** Status is `APPROVED`, with Reviewed By and Approved By giving the name, role and date of the sign-off, only when that document's `signedOff` is true. Otherwise Status is `DRAFT` and both are `[PENDING]`. The completion record is always `DRAFT` with both `[PENDING]`: nobody signs it.
4. **Name, ID and version agree.** The file name is the one `generate-document-id.mjs` gave, and the Document ID, the Version field and the Revision History's last row all carry the same version.
5. **A signed file was left alone.** If `status --json` showed `signOff.file` equal to the document's file before the write, that file is unchanged and the write went to the next minor version. A document nobody has signed off was rewritten in place. The completion record is always rewritten in place.
6. **The header is filled in honestly.** Owner is the profile's confirmed answer to who keeps the pack up to date, or `[PENDING]`. Any field nobody has given is `[PENDING]`, never a guess.
7. **The footer is there**, with Generated by, Generated on, ArcKit Version, Project and Model.
8. **Markdown rules apply outside the markers only.** Heading levels and table shapes between the markers are the helper's, and are never changed to satisfy a rule.

## Every worksheet a command edits

9. **`parse-worksheet --file` reports no problems** for every worksheet made or edited in this run, so no line starting `>` was changed and every field is where the helper reads it.
10. **A worksheet the helper printed was saved unchanged**, and no worksheet that already existed was made again.
11. **Each confirmed answer has a named person behind it.** Its `By` holds the name of the person who gave it in this conversation or in the request, and its `On` the date it was given. No name came from git, the plugin's configuration, a document or an earlier worksheet entry.
12. **Nothing was filled in that nobody gave.** Each question nobody answered is still `unanswered`, and the document prints it as **NOT YET ANSWERED**. Each answer taken from a file, or from a request that names nobody, is `proposed`, with `Source` saying where it came from.
13. **A method's status is the person's decision.** A method is `not-relevant` or `adapted` only where the person decided it, with their reason and their name in `Decided-by`, and, for `adapted`, what they do instead and how they have proved it works, in their words.
14. **The answers are the person's words.** The form may follow the question's hint (one item per line, `yes` or `no`), but no detail was added and nothing was dropped.

## Own methods and special processes

15. **Supplied text is word for word.** Each `Supplied-text` holds the business's written procedure exactly as supplied: same words, figures and order, with only the two-space indent that carries it on to more lines.
16. **No procedure was drafted, completed, corrected or improved**, even on request.
17. **`Process` names a special process or food exactly as the worksheet's guidance comment spells it.**
18. **`EH-review` is `pending`**, unless the person gave the date and outcome of the council's review.
19. **A `Limit` has the `Limit-source` the person gave for it**, and no figure or source was supplied by the command.

## Sign-off

20. **The helper recorded it.** `sign-off` exited 0 before the header was changed. On a refusal its messages were shown unchanged, and no worksheet, document or `sign-offs.json` was changed to work round it.
21. **The header matches the record.** Status, Reviewed By, Approved By and the Revision History row match `signOff` in `status --json`, and `check` still exits 0 after the edit.

## The completion record

22. **The unfinished list is the helper's.** The summary shows `status`'s `unfinished` lines unchanged, in their order.
23. **Nothing else changed.** No worksheet and no other document was changed by the check command.

## The summary

24. **It gives the counts from `status --json`** for the document: confirmed, proposed and not yet answered (or, for own methods, entries confirmed, proposed and unanswered).
25. **It says whether `check` passed**, with its message unchanged if it did not.
26. **It gives the header's status in plain words**: `DRAFT`, or signed off by the named person at the business, with their role and date.
27. **It names every proposed answer**, where it came from and who needs to confirm it.
28. **It carries the formatter note**: a program that reformats Markdown may rewrite the tables between the markers and make `check` fail, and the fix is to run the command again, not to edit by hand.
29. **It claims nothing for anyone.** It never calls the business, its food, a method or a document compliant, approved, safe or inspected, and never says or suggests that the Food Standards Agency or a council has reviewed, approved or endorsed anything. Not legal advice: the food business operator is responsible, and the council's environmental health team is the authority.
