# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Changed

- The profile and safe methods commands ask one question at a time, named by its worksheet reference, and record each answer before asking the next. "Skip" leaves a question for later. A safe method is named once, and a safety point shared by questions in a row is quoted once. The own methods command asks for one part of an entry at a time. The document is written again after each safe method, after the profile's questions about the business, and whenever the person stops (#1132).

- In Claude Code, the profile, safe methods and own methods commands put the questions that take yes or no in a selector, up to four at a time, each with the pack's wording. Choose an answer for each, or type what you do instead. An answer may be marked as recommended only where the pack sets out a method and asks whether you follow it, and nothing is recorded unless you choose it. Questions answered in your own words, and every other assistant, are unchanged (#1135).

- The README a new project folder is given lists all nine commands, in the order the overview guide gives them, and says that the diary and record sheets, like the completion record, are never signed off. A folder an earlier version set up is still taken as set up, and its README is left as it is.

### Fixed

- An error about a wrong value, such as a sign-off date still to come, prints its one-line message without the script's full usage text (#1132).

### Added

- The `uk-food-record-sheets` command, with a guide, writes the pack's blank diary and record sheets: the diary cover, the opening and closing checks with the business's own checks added once a named person confirms them in the Management safe methods, a week page to print or copy for each week, and the prove it records. It asks nothing about the forms, fills in no form, says no check has been done, and is never signed off: the pages are filled in, signed and kept by people at the business. Writing it again prints the forms afresh between the file's markers and leaves the rest of the file as it was, a note someone added there included, but for its Last Modified date and any sign-off cell holding something else, which it puts back to `DRAFT` or `[PENDING]` and names in its summary; once someone has typed into the forms it writes nothing over them until the person running it says that what was typed has been copied or is not wanted; a build, or a run with nobody there, never does. The `uk-food-sfbb` build recipe now also builds the allergen information, the cleaning schedule, the suppliers and contacts, and the diary and record sheets.
- The `uk-food-allergens` command, with a guide, records the business's allergen information: each dish against the fourteen allergens, one dish and one detail at a time. "Contains" is filled in only from a label, a product specification or a supplier's statement the business gives, or, for a single whole food with nothing added, from what the named person who looked at it says; "does not contain" only as the named person's own statement, once every ingredient is checked: in conversation, after the command has said which allergens it found named and which it did not, or in so many words in the message that starts it. Neither comes from a dish's name or from what a food usually holds, and the command looks nothing up. A "may contain" warning goes in the dish's notes, never in an allergen's answer. A dish counts only once a named person who is there has been shown it whole and agreed it: one given in conversation, or read from a file, is shown back whole; one set out in the message that starts the command, even in full by a named person, is recorded as stated and waits, and the summary reads it back whole, every allergen as recorded, as waiting to be shown and agreed. A "does not contain" nobody stated in the conversation, such as one typed into the worksheet by hand, is asked again before the dish is confirmed, after the command has said which it is clearing and why. An allergen nothing settles prints as NOT CONFIRMED, and the document is not signed off while one does. When a recipe, an ingredient, a brand or a supplier changes, each dish that may use it stops counting until the named person agrees it again, and a dish that does use it is checked again first. The command never says a dish is free from anything or suitable for anyone: a "free from" statement the business already makes is recorded in its own words, beside the pack's conditions for making one.
- Two commands for the business's own lists, each with a guide: `uk-food-cleaning-schedule` records the cleaning schedule on the pack's form, and `uk-food-suppliers` records the suppliers, the council's environmental health team and other contacts. Each takes one entry and one detail at a time in the business's own words. An entry given in conversation, or read from a file or a pasted list, is shown back whole and counts once a named person confirms it as shown; one a named person sets out in so many words in the message that starts the command, having said who they are, counts as given, and the summary reads it back whole for the person to check against what they sent. A business that keeps either list, or its allergen information, somewhere else can say where, and the document says so. A document finished that way, with nothing of the list confirmed in it, says that it holds nothing of that record and that nothing in it was checked against it, and status and the completion record say so too. Neither suggests anything for the list, and the suppliers command looks nobody up.
- The sign-off command signs off the allergen information, the cleaning schedule and the suppliers and contacts, and refuses the diary and record sheets, which nobody signs. Asked to sign "cleaning", it asks whether the Cleaning section of safe methods or the cleaning schedule is meant. The check command and the completion record cover the four new documents.
- First release of the UK Food Safety Overlay (experimental, community-maintained, England and Wales). It builds a small caterer's food safety documents from the Food Standards Agency's Safer food, better business for caterers pack: a business profile, the five sections of safe methods, the business's own methods, a completion record, and sign-off by a named person. Five commands do this: `uk-food-profile`, `uk-food-safe-methods` (one section at a time), `uk-food-own-methods`, `uk-food-check` and `uk-food-sign-off`. The profile, safe methods and own methods commands ask their questions in conversation, record an answer as confirmed only when a named person at the business gives or agrees it, and write their documents from the pack's wording and the business's answers, checked against a fresh print: a confirmed answer is printed as the business's own, and a proposed one is shown beside NOT YET ANSWERED with where it came from. The check command writes the completion record from the project as it stands, and the sign-off command records a named person's sign-off through the helper. A sign-off covers the whole document as it reads when it is signed: it ends when a line of that document changes, and a document whose header says it is signed off with no sign-off recorded fails the check.
- A guide for each command, written for the person running the business, and an overview guide on the ArcKit site covering the source pack, the order to work in, special processes and what the overlay does not cover.
- The `uk-food-sfbb` build recipe: the business profile, the five sections of safe methods, the business's own methods and the completion record, each target with a `min_lines` that suits its document, and no post-build health step. A build has nobody to answer, and its prompts give no named person's answers, so it confirms nothing: it writes the draft documents that do not exist yet, in which every unconfirmed answer reads NOT YET ANSWERED, and it never signs anything off.
