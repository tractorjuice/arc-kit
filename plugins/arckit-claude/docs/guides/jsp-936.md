# JSP 936: Dependable AI in Defence and how to evidence it

> **Guide Origin**: Official | **ArcKit Version**: [VERSION]

JSP 936 is the Ministry of Defence's policy for developing and using artificial intelligence safely and responsibly across defence. This guide covers who it applies to, its five ethical principles, how ethical risk sets the approval route, and how `/arckit:jsp-936` drafts the assurance evidence.

---

## What JSP 936 is

[JSP 936: Dependable Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Defence, Part 1: Directive](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/jsp-936-dependable-artificial-intelligence-ai-in-defence-part-1-directive) was published on GOV.UK on 13 November 2024 (version 1.1). GOV.UK calls it "the principal policy framework governing the safe and responsible adoption of AI in MOD".

- It builds on the [Defence AI Strategy](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/defence-artificial-intelligence-strategy) and the [Ambitious, Safe, Responsible](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ambitious-safe-responsible-our-approach-to-the-delivery-of-ai-enabled-capability-in-defence) policy, and says it is aligned to the Technology Code of Practice.
- Part 1 is the directive: what must be followed. Part 2 is the guidance that helps you comply. When Part 1 was published, the MOD said interim guidance, including a model card template and AI assurance question sets, was available internally.
- Where the JSP says "must", the action is compulsory. Where it says "should", it is recommended good practice.
- The MOD Director General Delivery & Strategy owns it. The Defence AI and Autonomy Unit (DAU) and the Defence AI Centre (DAIC) manage it.

## Who JSP 936 applies to

- It applies to "all Defence projects that include Artificial Intelligence (AI)", from the earliest point at which AI might be used.
- All MOD staff must apply it in every phase of the system life cycle, from pre-concept to disposal. It names research providers, capability sponsors, programme senior responsible owners, delivery teams, trials units, policy makers and end users among others.
- AI that is already running or being repurposed should be brought into line as far as possible. Where it can't, the JSP must be tailored and the extra risk managed.
- Meeting it "should be a shared endeavour between MOD and its suppliers". MOD teams must require AI suppliers to show they are competent, and the provenance of all training and test data must be assured and documented.
- Application should be proportionate to the maturity and nature of the AI.

The JSP gives examples. In scope: object recognition in intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance; reinforcement learning in command and control and autonomous systems; large language models in HR applications; AI in decision support tools. Out of scope: openly available search engines, predictive text on commercial messaging apps, and common office tools where the risk from the output is low.

## The five ethical principles

JSP 936 repeats the five principles from the Ambitious, Safe, Responsible policy:

| Principle | What it requires |
|-----------|------------------|
| Human-centricity | Assess the impact of the AI system on people, positive and negative, across the whole life cycle |
| Responsibility | Establish clear human responsibility and accountability for outcomes, and how human control is exercised |
| Understanding | Make sure the relevant people understand the system and its outputs, with that understanding designed in |
| Bias and harm mitigation | Proactively reduce the risk of unexpected or unintended bias or harm, at rollout and as the system learns, changes or is redeployed |
| Reliability | Show that the system is reliable, robust and secure |

## How ethical risk sets the approval route

You must carry out an AI ethical risk assessment at the start of a project and whenever a material change could alter the risk profile. Each AI use case gets an overall rating from impact and likelihood, based on residual risk: the risk with controls in place and working. The rating sets who must approve it (JSP 936 Table 1):

| AI ethical risk rating | Referral level for approval |
|------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Critical or Severe | 2PUS (the Second Permanent Under Secretary), or Ministers by exception |
| Major | Defence-level oversight, for example the Joint Requirements Oversight Committee / Investments Approvals Committee (JROC/IAC) |
| Moderate or Minor | Top Level Budget (TLB) oversight, delegated to business-level processes as appropriate |

- AI in kinetic effects and in novel or contentious applications needs special attention. Refer it to the DAU, notifying the DAIC, where the risk warrants top-level ownership.
- If a system presents unacceptable ethical risk, development or deployment must be halted safely until the risk is managed.
- Each TLB must appoint a Responsible AI Senior Officer (RAISO), and every AI project must identify its RAISO. SROs of medium or high risk AI projects must tell the RAISO about them.
- Large projects, projects with significant ethical risk, and teams that produce AI regularly should have at least an Ethics Manager and an Independent Ethics Assurance mechanism (para 105). This is recommended practice, not a must, and JSP 936 does not tie it to a Table 1 rating.
- TLB Executive Boards must give 2PUS an annual Statement of AI Ethical Assurance, backed by auditable evidence.

## Preparing JSP 936 evidence

### Before You Run

| Artefact | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| Requirements & architecture | Identify AI components, data flows, and human interaction |
| Risk register & security assessment | Seed ethical and safety risks |
| Data model & DPIA | Map personal/special category data |
| Governance roles | Confirm RAISO, Ethics Manager, project lead |

### Review Playbook

1. Validate risk classification and approval route with programme governance.
2. Confirm human-in-the-loop controls and escalation paths.
3. Check bias and harm mitigations include test coverage and ethical sign-off.
4. Ensure supplier assurance (if third-party AI) is documented, including data provenance.
5. Log required actions in backlog with owners and dates.

### Update Cadence

| Trigger | Action |
|---------|--------|
| New AI capability or model retraining | Re-run `/arckit:jsp-936` and update evidence |
| Risk classification change | Notify RAISO/Ethics board; revisit approval pathway |
| Annual assurance review | Refresh assessment; confirm monitoring data for the TLB's annual Statement of AI Ethical Assurance |

Keep signed copies and decisions as auditable evidence, retained in line with extant policy for software in your area.

## Using /arckit:jsp-936

`/arckit:jsp-936` drafts JSP 936 documentation for defence AI systems. Use it to evidence the five ethical principles, lifecycle coverage, and approval pathway.

### Command

```bash
/arckit:jsp-936 Generate JSP 936 assurance for <AI system>
```

Output: `projects/<id>/ARC-<id>-JSP936-v1.0.md`

### Assessment Structure

| Section | Focus | Key Evidence |
|---------|-------|--------------|
| Risk Classification | Likelihood × impact on residual risk (Critical → Minor) | Rating sets the referral level (2PUS, Defence-level, TLB) |
| Ethical Principles | Human-centricity, Responsibility, Understanding, Bias & Harm Mitigation, Reliability | Actions and mitigations per principle |
| Lifecycle Phases | Eight ArcKit phases, Planning → Quality Assurance | Evidence across planning, build, testing, deployment, monitoring. JSP 936 itself does not mandate a lifecycle: it sets lifecycle-agnostic requirements (section 6) and quality, safety and security requirements (section 7) |
| Governance | Roles, sign-off, battle rhythm | RAISO, Ethics Manager, assurance board dates |
| Continuous Monitoring | Drift detection, retraining policy, incident response | Links to `/arckit:servicenow` and ops runbooks |
