# Executive Summary Pattern

How an ArcKit artefact that asks its reader to accept a judgment or make a decision opens its Executive Summary. It applies to business cases, research findings, design reviews and any other artefact whose template carries a verdict; each such command points here from the step that writes the summary.

The pattern is Barbara Minto's Pyramid Principle: the answer first, then the reasons grouped under it, then the evidence under each reason. A board member who reads one sentence should leave with the decision.

## 1. The first sentence is the verdict

The first sentence under the Executive Summary heading states the verdict and what it asks of the reader: proceed or not, which option, approved or not, build or buy. Context comes after it, never before. If the verdict cannot be understood without one fact the reader may not hold, put that fact in the same sentence as a clause.

Do not open with what the document is, how the work was done, who took part or what was asked for. Those come after the reasons, or in Document Purpose.

## 2. Reasons, as many as the evidence supports

Under the verdict, give the reasons it rests on. Each reason:

- answers the question the verdict raises ("why this option?", "why these conditions?");
- is the same kind of idea as the reasons beside it: all decision criteria, or all cases of the Five Case Model, not a criterion next to a figure next to a process note;
- carries the figure or finding behind it.

Use the number the evidence gives. Three is a convention, not a rule: where a template shows three placeholder bullets, they show the shape, not a quota. Do not pad to fill them or merge real reasons to fit them. Order reasons by weight, most decisive first, unless the template fixes an order.

## 3. Risks and next steps close the summary

Risks, constraints, dependencies, conditions and next steps are not reasons. Keep them, at the end of the summary. The exception is a risk that would change the verdict if it materialised: state it in or next to the verdict sentence, not only in the closing list.

## 4. Nothing new

The summary reorders and condenses what the body of the artefact says. Every figure, option name, score and finding in it appears in the body or in a source the body cites. Do not round, combine or strengthen a figure on its way into the summary: "most requirements" does not become "all requirements", and a range of £1.2M to £1.6M does not become £1.4M.

## 5. Where it does not apply

- An artefact with no verdict: a glossary, a data model, a stakeholder map, an inventory. Keep the template's order.
- An artefact whose sequence is the content: a timeline, a runbook, an implementation plan, the project story. Time order is the logical order there.
- An inconclusive result. If the evidence supports no single verdict, say that first ("No option meets the affordability constraint; the board is asked to relax it or defer") rather than picking one.
