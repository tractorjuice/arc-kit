## LinkedIn Post Body

Is it legacy? The UK government changed how that question is answered in August, and ArcKit 6.19.0 helps you answer it for your own systems.

The updated definition of legacy IT from GDS and CDDO isn't about age. A system is legacy when keeping it running carries a burden or risk its owner finds unacceptable. It sets seven tests: support, contracts, skills, user needs, hardware, cyber security, and reliance on something that is itself legacy.

We've added an optional UK legacy overlay. Here's what it gives you:

→ A result on all seven tests for every system in scope: Accepted, Action-Required or Legacy, each backed by dated evidence.

→ Risk followed through dependencies, so a new application that relies on an unsupported system shows up too.

→ Honest gaps. Findings stay provisional until your organisation approves its thresholds, and missing evidence stays visible rather than counting as a pass.

→ The government's published risk score, kept separate from the classification, as the guidance does.

→ An action plan where every finding has an owner, a fix and a review date, ready for your business case and roadmap.

The overlay was contributed by Chris Nesbitt-Smith. The same release brings twelve security fixes from johnfelipe on GitHub that make ArcKit harder to fool: your published site, your files and what Claude reads are all better protected.

## Image Alt Text

ArcKit v6.19.0: Is it legacy? Assess existing systems against the UK's updated definition of legacy IT, with evidence and owned actions. Seven tests: out of support, missing or weak contracts, skills not there, can't meet user needs, unsuitable hardware, below cyber security minimums, relies on something legacy. Three outcomes: Accepted (within normal risk), Action-Required (legacy within 12 months without action), Legacy (unacceptable risk, fix it urgently). Risk follows dependencies: a new app relying on an unsupported system. A separate score: 16 or more out of 30 is red-rated, kept apart from the yes-or-no answer. Also, a harder harness: your site can't run code from your documents, edits can't be redirected onto credentials, untrusted text arrives as data.
